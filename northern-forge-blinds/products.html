Product categories

Blinds, shades, shutters & motorization

Four categories, each summarized below with what it is, what to weigh before choosing it, and a way to move straight into a consultation.

Blinds Shades Shutters Motorization

Blinds

Horizontal and vertical slat blinds tilt open and closed for precise, adjustable light control, and read as a clean, architectural line on a window.

Key considerations

  • Slat material and finish (aluminum, faux-wood, wood) affect durability and look.
  • Light gap between slats is a trade-off against full privacy when closed.
  • Moisture-prone rooms (bathrooms, kitchens) usually favor a non-wood slat.
  • Cleaning and dusting is an ongoing part of upkeep with slatted styles.

Shades

Roller, cellular (honeycomb), and Roman shades use continuous fabric instead of slats, giving a softer look and a wide range of opacities.

Key considerations

  • Opacity ranges from sheer, to light-filtering, to full blackout.
  • Cellular shades can add a measure of insulation at the window.
  • Cordless and motorized lift options address child-safety concerns.
  • Fabric shades are generally simpler to keep clean than slatted styles.
Start a Consultation About Shades

Shutters

Solid, louvered interior shutter panels are the most substantial and permanent of the four categories, and are typically framed to fit an exact window opening.

Key considerations

  • Panels are custom-fit to the window frame, so accurate measurement matters most here.
  • Material choice (wood vs. composite) matters more in humid rooms.
  • Shutters generally have a longer lead time than soft window treatments.
  • As a fixed, built-in feature, shutters are often considered a longer-term investment.
Start a Consultation About Shutters

Motorization

Motorization adds motor-driven operation to blinds, shades, or shutters, controlled by remote, wall switch, or app — rather than a cord or wand.

Key considerations

  • Power source: rechargeable/battery units versus hardwired options.
  • Motorization can often be retrofitted onto compatible existing treatments, or built into a new install.
  • App and schedule-based control can pair with a broader smart-home setup.
  • Typically the highest-cost option of the four categories; scope depends on the number of windows.
